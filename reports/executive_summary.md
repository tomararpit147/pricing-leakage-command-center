# Executive Summary: Pricing & Revenue Leakage Command Center

**Scope:** FY2024–2025 B2B transactions | 9,000 order lines | 260 customers | 8 SKUs across 4 product categories

---

## The headline number

Of **$615.6M** in list-price revenue, only **$530.0M (86.1%)** reaches the pocket. **$85.7M — 13.9% of list revenue — leaks out** across the discount and cost structure before it ever hits the margin line.

## Where the leakage happens

| Component | $ Leakage | % of Total Leakage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Invoice Discount (on-invoice, tier/channel-driven) | $62.0M | 72.3% |
| Freight | $11.8M | 13.7% |
| Payment Terms Cost of Capital | $6.6M | 7.7% |
| Co-op / Marketing | $3.7M | 4.3% |
| Volume Rebate | $1.6M | 1.9% |

**Takeaway:** nearly three-quarters of all leakage is happening at the invoice line — in the negotiated trade-discount grid itself — not in the "invisible" off-invoice items finance teams usually chase first. The discount grid is the highest-leverage lever, not freight or rebate policy.

## Where it concentrates (worst segments)

Leakage is not evenly spread. The five highest-leakage tier × channel × region combinations account for a disproportionate share of the total:

- **Tier 2 (Key) × Distributor × South** — 23.6% of list lost to leakage ($2.8M)
- **Tier 2 (Key) × Distributor × North** — 23.1% of list lost ($2.5M)
- **Tier 3 (Core) × Distributor × East** — 17.0% of list lost ($4.6M)
- **Tier 3 (Core) × Distributor × North** — 17.4% of list lost ($4.3M)
- **Tier 3 (Core) × Direct × North** — 10.1% of list lost ($3.4M)

**Takeaway:** Distributor-channel deals in the Tier 2/Tier 3 band are leaking at nearly double the rate of Direct or Tier 1 strategic accounts. This is the segment to target first — it has real volume behind it, unlike the long-tail Tier 4 accounts where leakage % is high but dollar exposure is small.

## What the market will bear (elasticity)

Across 12 logged historical price-change events spanning all 8 SKUs, every product showed **elastic demand** (|elasticity| > 1) — quantity moved more than proportionally with price. The portfolio-average elasticity from these events is **-0.81 to -1.0 range depending on product**, meaning price increases are not "free" — they come with a real, measurable volume trade-off, not just a margin one.

## What a price action would do

Applying the observed portfolio elasticity to a uniform **+5% price increase** across all segments:

- Projected pocket revenue: **+$4.0M** net margin impact
- A **-5% price decrease**, by the same arithmetic, would cost **-$6.1M** — the downside is larger than the upside because volume-elastic segments lose revenue faster than they gain it back in units

**Takeaway:** a targeted, segment-specific price action — not a blanket increase — captures more upside with less volume risk. The dashboard's Scenario Planner lets this be tested segment-by-segment rather than portfolio-wide.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Re-cut the Distributor trade-discount grid** for Tier 2/Tier 3 accounts — this is where 72% of leakage originates and where the volume base justifies the effort.
2. **Target a 3–5% price action on inelastic-leaning SKUs first** (see product-level elasticity table) to capture margin with minimal volume risk.
3. **Investigate payment-terms exposure** in the South and North regions, where 45–90 day terms are compounding the discount-grid leakage.
